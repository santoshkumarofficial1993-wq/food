Double-Crust Apple Pie

The two problems with apple pie are a soggy bottom and a two-inch gap between the filling and the lid. Both have the same cause — raw apples releasing water in the oven — and both are solved before the pie is assembled.

By Marguerite Bell Published 10 July 2025 Tested 6 times 4.9 ★ (1,533 ratings)

Active60 min
Bake65 min
Total3 hr
Slices8

Two apples, two jobs

A single-variety pie is always a compromise. Granny Smith holds its shape and brings acid but tastes thin on its own. Honeycrisp collapses into something close to apple sauce, which is exactly what you want filling the gaps between the firm pieces. Half and half by weight gives you both texture and body.

Other pairs that worked in testing: Braeburn with McIntosh, Northern Spy with Golden Delicious, and — the surprise of the round — Pink Lady on its own, which is the only single variety we would publish.

Why the filling is cooked first

Raw apples are roughly 84% water. Under a sealed lid, that water turns to steam, lifts the crust, then condenses back onto the bottom pastry. Cooking the filling on the stovetop for twelve minutes drives off the excess, sets the pectin so the slices keep their shape, and lets you taste and adjust the sugar before it is sealed inside a pie. It also means the pie is finished when the crust is browned, rather than needing another twenty minutes for the fruit to soften.

Six versions. The pre-cooked filling won on every measure except one — it takes twelve more minutes, and it is worth every one of them.

Ingredients

Tap any line to cross it off. Baked in a 9-inch pie plate — glass or metal, not ceramic.

All-butter pastry

  • 360 g plain flour (3 cups), plus more for rolling
  • 280 g cold unsalted butter, cut into 1 cm cubes and frozen 15 minutes
  • 1.5 tsp fine salt
  • 1 tbsp caster sugar
  • 120 ml ice water (½ cup), plus up to 2 tbsp more
  • 1 tbsp cider vinegar — slows gluten development, makes the dough forgiving

Filling

  • 900 g apples (about 6 medium), half Granny Smith, half Honeycrisp, peeled and cut into 1 cm slices
  • 150 g light brown sugar (¾ cup packed)
  • 25 g cornflour / cornstarch (3 tbsp)
  • 1.5 tsp ground cinnamon, 0.25 tsp ground allspice
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice and the zest of half a lemon
  • 30 g butter (2 tbsp)
  • 0.5 tsp fine salt

To finish

  • 1 egg beaten with 1 tbsp milk, for the wash
  • 2 tbsp demerara sugar for the top

Method

  1. Make the pastry

    Whisk flour, salt and sugar. Add the frozen butter and cut it in with your fingertips or a pastry blender until the largest pieces are the size of a pea and the rest is coarse crumbs. Visible butter is the goal — it becomes the flakes.

  2. Bring it together with as little water as possible

    Combine the ice water and vinegar. Add in stages, tossing with a fork, until the dough holds when squeezed but still looks shaggy. Divide in two, one piece slightly larger. Flatten into discs, wrap, refrigerate at least one hour or overnight.

  3. Cook the filling

    Toss the apples with sugar, cornflour, spices, lemon and salt. Cook in a wide pan over medium heat for 10–12 minutes, stirring, until the liquid thickens and the slices are just tender at the edge but still firm at the centre. Stir in the butter. Cool completely — at least 45 minutes. Warm filling melts the pastry butter on contact.

  4. Roll and line

    Roll the larger disc to a 30 cm circle on a floured surface. Lift it into the pie plate without stretching — stretched pastry shrinks back down the sides in the oven. Trim to a 2 cm overhang. Refrigerate 20 minutes.

  5. Fill and lid

    Pile the cooled filling in, doming it slightly. Roll the second disc and lay it over, or cut strips for a lattice. Trim, tuck the overhang under itself, and crimp firmly. Cut four steam vents if you went with a solid lid.

  6. Chill hard, then bake hot

    Freeze the assembled pie for 20 minutes — this is the step that guarantees a crisp bottom. Meanwhile heat the oven to 425°F / 220°C with a baking sheet on the lowest rack. Brush with egg wash, scatter demerara, and bake on that hot sheet for 20 minutes.

  7. Drop the temperature and finish

    Reduce to 375°F / 190°C and bake 40–45 minutes more, until the crust is deep brown — darker than instinct — and you can see the filling bubbling slowly through the vents. Shield the edges with foil if they run ahead.

  8. Cool four hours. Genuinely four.

    The starch needs to fall below about 120°F to set. Cut it warm and it runs across the plate. Four hours at room temperature on a wire rack; if you want it warm, reheat slices at 300°F for eight minutes.

Cook's notes

Pie plate material changes the bake

Glass and dark metal conduct well and give a properly browned base. Ceramic insulates, and in our tests the bottom crust was still pale after 70 minutes. If ceramic is what you own, blind-bake the base for 15 minutes first.

Making it ahead

Pastry discs keep three days refrigerated and three months frozen. The cooked filling keeps four days. A fully assembled unbaked pie freezes well for two months — bake from frozen, adding 20 minutes and starting under foil. A baked pie is best on day one, acceptable on day two, and a good breakfast on day three.

Altitude

Above 5,000 feet, reduce the sugar by 15 g and increase the initial bake to 25 minutes. Our Denver tester also found the filling needed an extra teaspoon of cornflour.
