Why our timings are boring and correct

Most recipe sites publish on the first successful cook. We publish on the second — and only if a second cook, working from the written words alone, gets the same result.

The process below is the whole reason this site exists. It is slow, it costs us roughly $41 in groceries per published recipe, and it is the only thing that separates a recipe you can trust from a recipe that photographs well.

How the test kitchen runs
  1. Step one

    Develop against a benchmark

    We cook the two or three best-known existing versions first, side by side, and write down exactly what is wrong with each.

  2. Step two

    Cook it blind, from the draft

    A second cook who was not in the development session follows the written draft with no verbal help. Every hesitation becomes an edit.

  3. Step three

    Weigh, time, photograph, publish

    Grams alongside cups, real elapsed clock time including preheat, and photos of the failure states — not only the perfect plate.
