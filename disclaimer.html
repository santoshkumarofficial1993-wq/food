The limits of what we can tell you — about nutrition, allergens, food safety and money. Worth five minutes, particularly the allergen section.

Last updated: 12 January 2026. This page forms part of our terms of use.

Nutrition information

Nutrition figures on recipe pages are estimates, calculated from USDA FoodData Central values for the specific brands and quantities we used during testing. They are provided because readers ask for them, and they carry real limitations:

  • Your ingredients will differ. Brand formulations, fat content of ground meat, size of produce and salt in stock all move the numbers, sometimes substantially.
  • Portion size is our judgement of a serving, not a nutritional prescription.
  • Cooking losses — fat rendered out and discarded, marinade not consumed — are estimated, not measured.
  • We are not registered dietitians and we do not employ one.

Treat the figures as a rough guide for comparison between recipes, not as a basis for medical or dietary decisions. If you are managing a condition where precise intake matters — diabetes, kidney disease, hypertension, an eating disorder, pregnancy — please work from advice given to you by a qualified professional who knows your situation.

Not medical or dietary advice

Nothing on this site is medical, nutritional or dietary advice, and nothing here should be used to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition. We do not publish weight-loss content, we do not sell diet plans, and we decline advertising from weight-loss products and supplements making health claims. If something you read here raises a health question, ask your physician or a registered dietitian.

Allergens

Our ingredient lists are accurate to what we cooked. They are not an allergen assessment, and we cannot make one:

  • Manufacturers change formulations without notice. A product that was gluten-free last year may not be today.
  • "May contain" and shared-line warnings vary by brand and by country.
  • Our test kitchen handles wheat, dairy, eggs, tree nuts, peanuts, soy, fish and shellfish. We cannot speak to cross-contamination in your kitchen.
  • Substitutions we suggest are tested for taste and texture, not verified for allergen safety.

If you or someone you cook for has a food allergy, read the label on every product you use, every time. Where a reaction could be serious, do not rely on any website, including this one.

Food safety

We publish target internal temperatures and follow USDA guidance for the recipes on this site. Cooking safely still depends on things we cannot see from here: the accuracy of your thermometer, the calibration of your oven, how long ingredients sat out, and the condition of what you bought.

  • Use a calibrated instant-read thermometer for poultry, pork, ground meat and stuffed dishes. Visual doneness is not reliable.
  • Refrigerate leftovers within two hours, one hour above 90°F.
  • Where a recipe involves raw or lightly cooked egg, we say so and we name the safer alternative.
  • People who are pregnant, very young, elderly or immunocompromised should follow the more conservative guidance from the FDA and USDA rather than ours.

Current official guidance is at foodsafety.gov, and it supersedes anything written here.

Cooking involves risk

Hot fat, sharp knives, boiling liquid, live flame and heavy cast iron are all genuinely hazardous. Some of our recipes — deep frying, high-heat searing, long smoking — carry more risk than others, and we flag the specific hazards on those pages. You cook at your own risk, and you are responsible for supervising anyone cooking with you. Advertising disclosure

This site carries display advertising served by Google AdSense and Google's certified partners. Advertising revenue funds roughly 44% of our operating costs. The rules we hold ourselves to:

  • Every advertising unit is visibly labelled "Advertisement".
  • A maximum of three units per page. No interstitials, no pop-ups, no auto-playing audio or video ads.
  • No advertisement is placed inside a method, between an ingredient list and its steps, or styled to resemble our own content, navigation or buttons.
  • We do not click our own ads or ask anyone else to, and we do not use any method to artificially inflate impressions or clicks.
  • We block advertising categories: alcohol, gambling, weight-loss and diet products, supplements with health claims, cryptocurrency and political advertising.
  • Advertisers have no input into editorial content. Not once, ever. If that changes, we will publish the fact.

Advertising is removed entirely for Kitchen Pass members. Personalised advertising cookies are set only with your consent — see our cookie policy.

Sponsored content

We accept a maximum of four sponsored recipe developments per year. Every one is labelled at the top of the page, in the feed and in the newsletter — never only in small print at the bottom. Sponsors approve factual claims about their own product and nothing else: not the recipe, not the rating, not the copy. If a sponsored product fails our testing we say so and refund the fee. Two of the last six were refunded. Sponsored recipes are permanently excluded from our "best of" round-ups.
