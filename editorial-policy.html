Editorial policy

The standards we hold ourselves to, written down so you can hold us to them as well. Where we have failed one of these, the failure is logged on the affected page.

Last updated: 12 January 2026.

1. Nothing is published untested

A recipe reaches this site only after a development cook and a blind verification cook by a second person, working from the written draft alone with no verbal help. If the second cook hesitates, asks a question, or produces a different result, the draft is wrong and gets rewritten — not clarified in conversation.

About one recipe in nine fails verification. Eleven have been abandoned entirely because we could not make them work reliably in a home kitchen; those write-ups are published rather than quietly dropped.

This rule holds under deadline. It has held through every holiday season since 2016. If a Thanksgiving recipe is not ready, it publishes in December.

2. How recipes are written

  • Weights first, volumes alongside. Grams and ounces, then cups and spoons.
  • Real elapsed time, including preheating and resting. Our "total time" is what a stopwatch reads, not the sum of the active steps.
  • Visual and sensory cues in every step. What it looks like, sounds like and smells like — because ovens vary and clocks do not know that.
  • One instruction per step. If a step contains two actions, it gets split.
  • Failure states described. What it looks like when it is going wrong, and what to do about it.
  • The story stays short. Context that helps you cook goes in; childhood memory for word count does not.
  • Substitutions are tested or labelled untested. We never suggest a swap we have not cooked.

3. Regional attribution

Before we label a dish as belonging to a region it must pass at least two of three tests: a documented pre-1980 printed source placing it there; ingredient logic tied to what the region actually had; and living practice — people there still cook it at home in roughly this form.

When a dish is popularly associated with a region but fails all three, we say so on the page rather than repeating the claim. There are eleven of those, and they are among our most-read articles.

Where a dish comes from a culture none of us belongs to, we commission a contributor who does, and we pay them. We do not "adapt" our way into a tradition we have not cooked in.

4. Corrections

Every correction is logged publicly at the bottom of the page it affects, with a date and a plain description of what changed. We do not silently edit. Sixty-one recipes have been corrected this way, most of them after a reader wrote to us.

If a correction is significant enough to change a result — a wrong temperature, a missing ingredient, an incorrect quantity — it also goes in the next Thursday email, whether or not that is a comfortable thing to send.

Corrections take priority over everything else in our inbox and usually get a same-day reply.

5. Artificial intelligence

No recipe on this site was generated by an AI system. Every one was developed by a person who cooked it, tested by a second person who cooked it again, and written by a person. We use software for spellchecking and for nutrition estimation, and that is the full extent of it.

We do not publish AI-generated articles, AI-generated photography, or AI-written product recommendations, and we do not accept placements of AI-generated content from partners. If this ever changes, it will be disclosed at the top of the affected page, not in a footnote.

6. Equipment testing

  • Every test unit is bought at retail, with our own money.
  • Free samples are not accepted in exchange for coverage. Unsolicited products are returned or donated.
  • The protocol is written and fixed before the units arrive, so criteria cannot be adjusted to fit a preferred result.
  • Minimum three months of daily use before anything is called a winner.
  • Results are measured, not felt, and the raw numbers are published even when they are dull.
  • Losers are named, with the reason. Our equipment articles are long because of this.
  • Everything is retested annually. Manufacturers change specifications quietly; two former winners have been removed for exactly that reason.

No amount of advertising spend places a product into a test round, and appearing in a test round requires no spend.

7. The advertising firewall

  • Advertisers never see recipes before publication, unless they have paid for a sponsored recipe — and then only their own.
  • No advertiser has ever caused a recipe to be changed. If that ever happens, we will publish the fact.
  • Sponsored content is labelled at the top of the page, in the feed, and in the newsletter. Never only at the bottom, never only in small print.
  • Sponsored recipes are permanently excluded from "best of" round-ups.
  • A maximum of four sponsored developments per year. If the product fails our testing we say so and refund the fee — two of the last six were refunded.
  • Affiliate relationships are disclosed above the products they relate to, not buried in a footer.
  • We block advertising categories: alcohol, gambling, weight-loss and diet products, supplements with health claims, cryptocurrency and political advertising.

Because no single advertiser exceeds about 2% of our revenue, we can afford to decline — and we decline roughly forty requests a month. That structural independence is the point of publishing our funding breakdown on the about page.

8. Health claims and nutrition

We publish estimated nutrition figures because readers ask for them, with the limits set out in our disclaimer. We do not publish weight-loss content, diet programmes, or claims that a food prevents, treats or cures anything. We are not qualified to, and the category is full of harm. This also applies to what we will sell: no diet plans, no supplements, no "detox" anything.

9. Recipes stay free

Every recipe published on this site remains free to read in full, permanently, with no account required and no registration wall. Membership buys convenience and removes advertising. It has never gated a recipe and it never will. This is the commitment we would find hardest to walk back, which is exactly why it is written here.

10. Reader contributions

We do not accept unpaid recipe submissions or guest posts. When we want work from someone outside the team, we commission it and pay for it. Reader corrections, requests and independently run test results are welcome and are credited by name when published, unless you ask us not to.

11. Who decides

The editor has final say on published copy. The head of testing has final say on whether a recipe is ready. Neither role reports to whoever sold the advertising, because nobody here sells advertising as their main job — enquiries go through a shared inbox and are answered against the rules above.

Holding us to this

If you think we have broken one of these standards, write to hello@skilletandstars.com with the page and what you saw. We will reply, and if you are right, the correction gets logged publicly like any other.
