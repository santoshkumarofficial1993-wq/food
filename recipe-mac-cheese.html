Baked Mac and Cheese, Three Cheeses

A casserole that holds a clean edge when you cut it, stays creamy underneath and gets a top you can hear. The béchamel does the structural work; the three cheeses each do one job and nothing else.

By Ray Okonkwo Published 12 July 2025 Tested 4 times 4.9 ★ (2,041 ratings)

Prep20 min
Bake35 min
Total55 min
Serves8

The three cheeses and what each one does

Most baked mac recipes name a cheese blend and leave you guessing why. Here is the actual division of labour, established by cooking the same sauce eleven times with single-cheese variations:

  • Sharp cheddar — flavour. Two-year aged if you can get it. On its own it splits under heat and the sauce turns grainy, which is why it is never the only cheese in a good version.
  • Gruyère — body and browning. It carries the nutty edge and browns evenly on top. Comté or a young Emmental substitute cleanly.
  • American cheese — insurance. 115 g of it, and it is not a shortcut. The sodium citrate in processed slices keeps the emulsion stable through 35 minutes of oven heat. Leave it out and the sauce breaks about one time in three.

Grate everything yourself. Pre-shredded bags are coated in potato starch and cellulose to stop clumping, and that coating thickens the sauce unpredictably — it was the single biggest source of failure in reader reports before we added this note.

The béchamel is not old-fashioned fussiness. It is the only way to hold a cheese sauce at 375°F for half an hour without it separating.

Ingredients

Tap any line to cross it off. Baked in a 9×13 inch dish.

Pasta

  • 450 g elbow macaroni (1 lb) — or cavatappi, which holds more sauce
  • 1 tbsp kosher salt for the pasta water

Sauce

  • 60 g unsalted butter (4 tbsp)
  • 45 g plain flour (6 tbsp)
  • 900 ml whole milk, warmed (3¾ cups) — cold milk lumps the roux
  • 340 g sharp cheddar, grated (12 oz)
  • 170 g Gruyère, grated (6 oz)
  • 115 g American cheese, torn (4 oz, about 6 slices)
  • 1 tsp English mustard powder
  • 0.25 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
  • Kosher salt, white pepper, cayenne to taste

Top

  • 70 g panko breadcrumbs (1¼ cups)
  • 30 g butter, melted (2 tbsp)
  • 40 g Parmesan, finely grated
Gluten-free and dairy-free versions For gluten-free, use a 1:1 GF flour blend for the roux and GF elbows cooked two minutes under the box time — the sauce behaves identically. Dairy-free is harder and we will not pretend otherwise: cashew-based cheeses do not emulsify the same way. The closest we got used oat milk, a starch-thickened base and nutritional yeast, and it is a genuinely different dish. We have written it up separately rather than calling it a substitution.

Method

  1. Heat the oven, butter the dish

    375°F / 190°C, rack in the upper third. Butter a 9×13 inch baking dish generously — the corners are where it sticks.

  2. Undercook the pasta on purpose

    Boil the macaroni in well-salted water for two minutes less than the box says. It will finish in the oven, absorbing sauce as it goes. Fully cooked pasta turns to mush by minute 25 of baking. Drain, do not rinse.

  3. Make the roux

    Melt the butter over medium heat, whisk in the flour, and cook for exactly 90 seconds — long enough to lose the raw-flour taste, short enough that it stays blond. It should smell like biscuits, not toast.

  4. Build the béchamel

    Add the warm milk in three additions, whisking each one smooth before the next. Simmer gently for 5 minutes, stirring, until it coats the back of a spoon and leaves a clean line when you draw a finger through it. Season with mustard powder, nutmeg, salt, white pepper and a pinch of cayenne.

  5. Melt in the cheese off the heat

    Take the pan off the burner. Add the cheese in three handfuls, stirring each until fully melted before adding more. Direct heat at this stage is what breaks the sauce — residual heat is plenty. Hold back 85 g of the cheddar for the top.

  6. Combine and taste properly

    Fold pasta into sauce. It will look far too loose. That is correct — the pasta drinks roughly a quarter of it in the oven. Taste now and season harder than feels right; baking mutes salt noticeably.

  7. Top and bake

    Spread into the dish. Toss panko with melted butter and Parmesan, scatter over with the reserved cheddar, and bake 30–35 minutes until the top is deep gold and the edges bubble. If the top browns before the centre is hot, tent with foil.

  8. Rest 15 minutes

    Non-negotiable if you want a clean edge on the plate. Straight from the oven it pours; after 15 minutes it holds. The middle is still well above serving temperature.

Cook's notes

Making it ahead

Assemble completely, cool, cover and refrigerate for up to two days. Add 12–15 minutes to the bake and start it covered. Do not freeze it assembled and unbaked — the sauce separates on thawing. Baked and frozen portions reheat acceptably at 325°F with a splash of milk stirred through.

Additions that survived testing

  • Smoked ham hock or bacon, 200 g, rendered and folded in — the most requested variation, and it works.
  • Roasted broccoli, added after the pasta so it does not steam and go grey.
  • Hot sauce, 1 tbsp of a vinegar-forward one, stirred into the sauce. Lifts the whole dish.
  • Caramelised onion, 200 g. Excellent, but adds 45 minutes to the recipe and it is not really Southern any more.

Additions that did not

Truffle oil (bitter after baking), cream cheese (pasty texture), and evaporated milk in place of the béchamel — that last one makes a fine stovetop mac, but it does not set in the oven and it browns unevenly. We have that stovetop version published separately.
